**Why Canada had Florena: the NIVEA trademark-expropriation arc, ESTABLISHED
(maker-primary: beiersdorf.com "international development" history page,
read this run).** After WWII almost all Beiersdorf affiliates were seized
as enemy property and the NIVEA trademarks were sold off country by
country; Beiersdorf spent 50+ years buying them back — the process closed
with the Polish Nivea/Lechia repurchase in 1997, when Beiersdorf "again
owns the NIVEA trademarks worldwide." In Canada, Beiersdorf's Nivea-formula
products were sold under the Florena name while the Canadian Nivea mark
was out of Beiersdorf hands; once recovered, Beiersdorf Canada rebranded
Florena → Nivea. USS-SpongeBob's 2019-06-29 SOTD note (sotd_2019_full.json)
is the dataset's primary witness: "I shaved with this cream back when it
was still called Florena in Canada before Beiersdorf rebranded it as
Nivea" (his 2019 field = Nivea Sensitive Skin Shaving Cream, nivea.ca
product link). Confidence: brand-switch established (dataset note +
trademark mechanism); the exact Canadian recovery year is unpinned
(post-1997 window only). Learned 2026-10-30.
