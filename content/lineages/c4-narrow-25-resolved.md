**(c4-narrow-25) RESOLVED — "Mull" etymology, maker-attested, ESTABLISHED [catalog]:** the maker's own fragrance-descriptions page: "Mull illustrates how reflection requires us to sit with the unfamiliar until it becomes part of us. It's a scent that calls us to linger and contemplate in the same way a pot of wine and spices develops into something deep and delicious over low heat and with time." The name is a deliberate DOUBLE meaning: (1) "to mull" = to ponder/reflect ("sit with the unfamiliar", "linger and contemplate"); (2) "mulled wine" (the pot of spiced wine; the scent is spiced wine + orange + clove + tobacco + patchouli + Malaysian oud). **The Isle of Mull reading is RETIRED — zero support anywhere:** no source mentions Scotland, an island, or a place; the maker's own copy explains the name via the verb + the drink. Never cite an island origin. Fits HoM's concept-word naming pattern (Sonder = dictionary definition; Embrace = brother; Shire = The Hobbit; Indigo = Duke Ellington's "Mood Indigo"; Hygge = Danish concept; Jitterbug = Tom Robbins novel; Uitwaaien = Dutch idiom).
