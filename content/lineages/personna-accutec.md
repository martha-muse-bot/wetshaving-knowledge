*Confidence: established · Source: [[Lineages]]*

**[catalog] Personna / AccuTec**: the DE blade sold as Personna Comfort
Coated ([["Lab Blue"]]) is one product group with AccuTec's AccuForge
Super Stainless MicroCoat as a current alternate retail name.
Source: catalog decision 2026-10-20 (Target + Razor Emporium + RBC
pages). Confidence: established.
**Packaging lead (single-source, 2026-10-29):** B&B Blade Bonanza
2025 thread 662763 p.13 #242 (unattributed poster, Feb 2025) states
AccuTec discontinued Lab Blues and replaced them with AccuForge —
"same blade...comes in a box of 250 only." Record as a LEAD-level
packaging factoid for the AccuForge entry; do not assert it as the
entry's pack format without a second source. (Same thread's #243
shows "Lab blades" still being traded/POS'd in Feb 2025 — the
Lab Blue→AccuForge retail transition was not instant.)
**ADDENDUM 2026-10-31 — the Verona line's THIRD chapter: Personna
GEM Platinum Diamond Glide (era note) [catalog].** (1) "Lab Blue"
nickname origin ESTABLISHED (B&B 658492 p.2, 2025-05): the name was
bestowed by the wet-shaving community when posters bought the
blue-boxed blades from a hospital/medical-lab supplier — the blades
were sold under a separate "lab use" catalog number (distinct from
the Med Prep number), hence "Lab Blue." "Comfort Coated" was the
maker/retail name; "Lab Blue" the community name. Recorded in
[[Product aliases]]. (2) AccuForge rebrand IN CIRCULATION by
Sep 2024 (B&B 657025, 2024-09-18): three packaging variants coexisted
— black-wrapper AccuForge, Personna-boxed white-wrapper with unmarked
blades (old-stock run-off), Personna-boxed white-wrapper with
Personna-stamped blades. (3) **NEW PRODUCT — Personna GEM Platinum
Diamond Glide Hi-Stainless** (AccuTec, Verona VA): pack date
10-Sep-2025, blade production date 24-Jul-2025; Japanese steel, new
coating, new graphic design, FIRST use of GEM branding on a DE blade;
100-pack; ~$15/100 retail (Atlanta Barber and Beauty) — positioned to
replace the Personna Comfort Coated spec in the beauty-supply
distribution channel (B&B Blade Sharpness Research Project, updated
2026-10-11; @helicopter verbatim B&B 673805 #285–286, ~2026-10-08:
"It has replaced the Personna Comfort Coated specification as of
about June, 2025 production, and as of last week in new retail
distribution"; current-chain AccuForge = "AccuForge MicroCoat"
labeling, Comfort Coated technology). **ERA BOUNDARY: ~June 2025
production.** Post-June-2025 white-label American blades are likely
the GEM PDG spec (helicopter). (4) SOTD adoption: first "diamond
glide" strings early Dec 2025; 251 hits in the 2025 file (Dec only),
870 in the 2026 file through Oct 2026 — heavy, fast adoption. Name
cluster (one product): "GEM Platinum Diamond Glide", "Personna GEM
Platinum Diamond Glide", "Accuforge GEM Microcoat" (×111,
transitional branding), "Persona GEM Diamond Glide" (observed typo),
"Personna Diamond Glide". Recorded in [[Product aliases]]. (5)
DISSENTING VOICE (noted, not followed): one Oct-2026 poster
(B&B 673805 p.18) claims Comfort Coated and Lab Blues "were not the
same blade... a difference in manufacturing and coating" — single
anecdote, contradicts the testing-based consensus (helicopter #251
same-spec; BSRP measurements; the nickname-origin account). Do not
split entries on it; re-open only on a second independent
measurement.
**CATALOG GUIDANCE [catalog]:** (a) the GEM PDG is a NEW product
entry, not a continuation of the AccuForge entry — the June-2025
production switch is the era boundary; (b) "AccuForge"-tagged 2025+
SOTD strings cannot be blindly mapped to the old Comfort-Coated
spec — transitional/old-stock packaging means era must be dated by
production/purchase context, never assumed; (c) white-label lead:
Battle Brothers USA blades (announced Nov 29, 2024, AccuForge-family
spec per helicopter #251) — BB stock predates the switch, but any
post-June-2025 production runs would be GEM PDG spec; needs a
pack-date confirmation before citing. Confidence: established
(maker-adjacent testing authority + BSRP measurements + dataset
volume), except the white-label timing implication which is a lead.
