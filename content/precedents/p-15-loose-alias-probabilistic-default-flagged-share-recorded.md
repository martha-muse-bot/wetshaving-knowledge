*Status: standing · Source: [[Precedents]]*

**Statement:** When a string is ambiguous among KNOWN, REAL products and carries
no discriminating signal, it may resolve to the most-used candidate as a LOOSE
alias: the match is flagged probabilistic (never presented as certain), and the
alias records its basis — the leader's share among identified uses of that
family. No dominance floor: the recorded share is the disclosure. Reports show
loose-alias counts as a distinct probable sub-line, and the assignment unwinds
if real evidence arrives. Loose aliases never settle IDENTITY disputes (whether
a distinct product exists — that stays P-11 unresolved).
**Established by:** user ruling on §B7 "Shark (Egypt)" (2026-10-21) — his
argument: bare "Astra" → SP rests on base rates alone, and "(Egypt)" is the
same shape of problem; the binary alias-or-nothing treatment was inconsistent.
**UNIVERSAL APPLICATION (user, 2026-10-21):** ALL likelihood-based defaults
are loose — including the former P-07 popularity defaults (bare "Astra" → SP
first among them: "We aren't confident they used Astra SP, it's just very
very likely"). P-07 survives only as the leader-selection rule; its outputs
are applied as P-15 loose aliases. What stays HARD: resolutions where the
string's own semantics pick exactly one candidate (§B5 "Perma-Sharp
Stainless" — the brand's only DE blade; "Shark Stainless" — the line's only
stainless-named product), per-post razor-format resolution (P-14), and
proven author-scoped aliases (§B4).
**Boundary (user, 2026-10-21, §B4):** the premise is NO discriminating signal —
and a conspicuously OMITTED discriminator is signal. Posters who mean Gillette
Silver Blue write the full name or "GSB"; a bare "Gillette Blue" from an
unproven author drops the key word and points away from Silver Blue, so the
§B4 residue does NOT loose-alias (it stays ambiguous). Base rates computed
from full-name writers do not transfer to a self-selected shorthand
population whose shorthand omits the discriminator.
**Relied on by:** §B7 "Shark (Egypt)" → Shark Super Stainless (share computed
from identified Shark uses); §B8 bare "ASCO" → Super Stainless (2026-10-21);
§B9 bare "Gillette Winner" → Winner Platinum (2026-10-21); bare "Feather"
in an AC-razor post → AC Professional (2026-10-21; within-format loose alias
under the P-14 hard constraint — the Professional is the lineup's standard
per Feather's distributor Jatai, ProGuard second).
