*Confidence: established · Source: [[Product aliases]]*

`[catalog] "Personna travel" is a descriptor, NOT a product — RESOLVED
dataset mystery (2026-10-21).** The backlog's "Personna 'travel' /
`person a travel (aka bbs-0)`" question resolves against the
poster's own words. All four strings are from ONE poster,
Enndeegee (2020): `personna travel vintage`, `personna travel
aluminum` (×2), and `person a travel (aka bbs-0)` (2020-06-21).
His note reads in full: he bought "a quirky razor" cheap "semi
blind" on eBay, "a someone on here IDed it for me," and "it
appears to have been given away as a travel razor and at one
point some people thought it was the precursor for the BBS-1" —
i.e. he named it for its PROVENANCE (an airline-amenity giveaway),
not for a product name, and the parenthetical "(aka bbs-0)"
identifies it as the New Angle 70 itself. Corroborating
construction: `personna travel aluminum` matches the New Angle
70's documented aluminum handle (Sharpologist, 2022). By
2020-12-24 Enndeegee posts the razor as `personna bbs-0` — the
same poster renames his own razor once the community ID is
confirmed. External corroboration of the travel provenance: the
B&B vintage thread's hedged "first-class airline passengers
flying out of England" note on the New Angle 70 (lead, recorded
in [[Hardware lore]]), plus wegianwetshaving's "Airline Tech"
(2023-04) documenting airline giveaway kits of vintage English
razors (a Gillette example — the practice existed, not the
Personna). No distinct "Personna travel" product turns up
anywhere (no maker listing, no packaging). **METHOD (for the
matcher): provenance-as-product-name — posters sometimes write
the razor's provenance ("travel") where a model name would go;
such descriptor strings fold INTO the evidenced product (here
Personna New Angle 70), never spawn a new entry.**
Source: dataset strings + full notes in sotd_2020_full.json;
B&B "Focus on Vintage DEs" thread post 2021-04-03;
wegianwetshaving.com/2023/04/airline-tech/. Confidence:
established (identity = New Angle 70); the airline-amenity
provenance itself remains a community-sourced lead.
