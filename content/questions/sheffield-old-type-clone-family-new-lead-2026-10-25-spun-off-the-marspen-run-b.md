*Status: open · Source: [[Open questions]]*

Sheffield Old-Type-clone family (new lead, 2026-10-25, spun off the
Marspen run): B&B Souplex 636564 p.6 #104 claims Sheffield-made
Gillette Old Type clones were produced "for many companies and
stores over a very long period" — brands in the family incl. Best
British, Laurel (George H. Lawrence), and dept-store versions
(Marspen). Open: did Wilkinson Sword actually produce any of
them, or is the Laurel look the whole story? A family-level entry
would firm up Marspen, Best British, and any future store-brand
open combs at once; needs Waits' Razor Compendium or a
collector's Sheffield-clone survey, not single-forum guesses.
**PARTIALLY RESOLVED 2026-10-25 ([[Hardware lore]] addendum):**
family = real PATTERN, not a proven maker chain. Laurel firmed
up as a major Sheffield maker (G.H. Lawrence Ltd, Nursery St /
Laurel Works; 1M razors to French troops 1940; rebuilt 1948,
trading to early 1970s; Dumb Bell 1934, Ladies Boudoir 1935–40)
— plausible pattern origin, NOT proven maker of Best British /
Marspen. Wilkinson leg WEAKENED (London SE maker, Pall Mall
1896 — no DE open-comb product found; Sheffield not a Wilkinson
plant town). Best British = brand in two tiers (cheap litho-tin
vs heavy brass silver-plate, B&B #103) but ZERO dataset razor
strings; Laurel = 23 strings / 1 author (Enndeegee). Marspen→
Best British link still pure speculation. CORRECTION: Souplex is
Morecambe, Lancashire (V&A M.59-1984), NOT Sheffield — outside
the family. Remaining: a Waits/collector survey or cap-stamp
photos to prove any specific maker link; Wilkinson DE open-comb
product, if one exists, would rehabilitate that leg.
NEW LEADS from this run: (1) Enndeegee's "Laurel open comb"
(23 posts, 2020–2022) — which Laurel model is it (Dumb Bell?
another full-size open comb?), and does a second owner exist
to lift it past single-author?;
**PARTIALLY RESOLVED 2026-10-26 ([[Hardware lore]] addendum):**
still single-author (re-verified: 23 posts / 1 author, 2020-09
to 2022-12), but the model narrows to the Dumb Bell BY
ELIMINATION (lead, not established): Enndeegee's razor takes
standard modern DE blades only (Feather, GSB, 7 O'Clock Yellow,
Rapira, Voskhod — blade fields), which rules OUT the Ladies
Boudoir (miniature, proprietary tiny blades, slotted guard
with no comb teeth); the Dumb Bell is the only documented
full-size Laurel DE, and new primary-ad evidence puts it in
retail 1938–39 (Chemist and Druggist, nickel-plated 6d, Geo.
H. Lawrence Ltd, Reg. No. 794-132). BLOCKER: no source yet
calls the Dumb Bell open-comb, and B&B 505224 #16 says
Dumb-Bell blades were a sought-after NOS item interchangeable
with Wardonia/Knockout/Ocean blades — whether that Sheffield
blade family is standard 3-hole or proprietary is unpinned.
Settles on a Dumb Bell head photo or Waits entry. (2) Wilkinson Sword DE
open-comb hunt — did Wilkinson ever make/sell a DE open comb
in the 1930s/40s (store-brand or own-brand), or is the #104
claim simply wrong?;
**RESOLVED 2026-10-26 ([[Hardware lore]] addendum) — claim
UNCORROBORATED, weighted UNLIKELY, as far as text research
can go.** Wilkinson's documented own-brand razors contain no
DE open comb at any era (Pall Mall 1898 + Empire 1929–1952
are SE wedge; 1970 Bonded is cartridge; modern Classic is a
closed-comb DE); targeted searches surface no Wilkinson DE
open comb, product, listing, or second collector. CORRECTION
recorded: Wilkinson DID have a Sheffield cutlery address
(Arundel Lane, straight-razor-era directory lists), so the
"no Sheffield plant" line was too flat — but a directory
address is not a DE factory and does not rehabilitate the
claim. BONUS: Wilkinson's real later DE is the 1960s "Sticky"
(dataset strings 2019–2025; Kenneth Grange design lead,
Design Museum confirms Grange designed Wilkinson razors
over decades) — its own entry, never cited in the #104
thread; its comb type unpinned. Re-open ONLY on a
Wilkinson-named cap stamp or a Wilkinson DE-open-comb
catalog/ad listing. (3) Souplex maker history as its own
thread (Morecambe firm: founded when, Big Ben c.1935, Double
Six/National/Knockout/Nymph line, end date) — now that Souplex
is confirmed outside the Sheffield family, its own entry
needs building before validators meet more Souplex strings.
**PARTIALLY RESOLVED 2026-10-26 ([[Hardware lore]] entry):**
Souplex Ltd, Morecambe, documented by Sept 1925 (C&D blade
ads, 2/6 per dozen, wholesale-only); Big Ben travel razor
c.1935 (V&A M.59-1984); Nymph ladies' set by Sept 1935 (3/6);
Shaving Senior set + Bakelite toothbrush (July 1936); Double
Six differential-aggression Bakelite DE (pre-1944 example,
Minor metal-cap variant late 1940s); pepper-pot cases early
1930s–late 1950s-ish (soft tail, not an end date).
**CORRECTION to this item's framing: Knockout is NOT a
Souplex line** — Knockout = Cartledge commission made by
Cooper & Sons (Fuluse rebrand); National IS Souplex and
Nelson is the same design in brown (Harrison Fisher, lead).
Design family shared, makers differ; see the hardware_lore
entry for the thread evidence. **[[Leads left]]:** founding year;
firm end date; National production dates; Nelson maker
confirmation; United Plastics vs Souplex as the
Fuluse/Knockout shell moulder (theory only); Double Six
launch date (pre-1944 bound only). **Also recorded:**
