**The story (B&B 571174 p.4, "Tradere Is Back", published 2019-07-28, updated 2020-08-18, read verbatim):** "Except for the reversible base plate they are identical" (Blackland Razors/Shane); "The SBS and Tradere parts are identical down to tool markings"; "Ikon also stopped making the SBS due to potential patent infringement"; the SBS came after Tradere left the market; the same-machinist speculation was REJECTED (Blackland confirmed with the Tradere machinist); Rockwell's flippable-plate patent is an alternative infringement theory (speculation only). **Two generations:** (1) original **solid brass**, no serial numbers, <500 made (kingfisher's educated guess — Greg was asked many times, never answered), sold over $200 new, value went up; OC side "likely modeled after the Gen 2 Tradere" (kingfisher, hedged); the reversible-baseplate flip is a subtle difference on the brass original but discernible on the new one. (2) the new SBS is **aluminum/aluminum alloy** ("Greg said it was made of the same material that the Shavecraft 101 is made from"), "a completely different beast... certainly INSPIRED by the Tradere and the original SBS, but it has enormous differences" — kingfisher: "I wish Greg had not named that razor 'SBS,' too, as though it were the same head made of a different material. It's not." One dissenting voice (guitarslinger): "pretty sure the non-brass SBS is injection molded stainless steel like the Supply and Rockwell razors... It's a boat anchor." Connaught sold SBS heads (£20 ex VAT clearance). Shavecraft heads are aluminum/alloy, not stainless (kingfisher: threads easily perturbed suggests aluminum; "nobody has come to the conclusion that these are stainless steel").
