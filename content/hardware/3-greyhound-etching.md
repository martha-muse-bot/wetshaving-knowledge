**(3) Greyhound etching — PARTIALLY RESOLVED: greyhound = older,
unetched = newer is a HEDGED firsthand observation; era-tracking
UNPROVEN [catalog].** GAW9576 (B&B 576746 #8): "I have noticed a
difference between the old blades with the greyhound etched on them
and the presumably newer blades with no greyhound" — "presumably"
is his hedge, not a dated fact. Flintstone65 (#24) adds a material
anecdote, not a date: his non-greyhound blade's ear snapped while
making a carrier; "My Greyhound etched blade is still good."
CAUTION: "unetched" conflates two populations — jmudrick (#10)
notes the ill-fitting unbranded substitute blades are probably not
Enders production at all ("I don't presume the ill fitting blades
are even Enders production"), so an unetched blade is not
automatically a later genuine Enders blade. NO source ties the etch
to Simmons-era vs Durham-Enders-era production, and no
greyhound-trademark/logo source was found anywhere (the registered
Enders mark per the 1936 opinion is the "Wm. Enders" facsimile
signature, No. 74,158 — no dog). Do NOT date a blade by the etch,
and do NOT cite the etch as a Simmons/Durham-Enders era marker.
THIRD BLADE VARIANT (Ron R #6): some Enders blades "have a relief
cut inwards" — purpose unknown ("Experimenting?"), recorded here
so a future photo-read has the variant on file.
RESIDUAL LEAD: the 1955 end-of-life ad (Bonham Daily Favorite,
Jul 17, 1955, posted by jmudrick #10, "an ad from the last days of
the razor... with blades pictured") — a photo read of the pictured
blades would show whether 1955-era blades carried the greyhound;
live-browser job (B&B attachment 1033896).
