*Confidence: established · Learned: 2026-10-24 · Source: [[Hardware lore]]*

**[catalog] Gillette "Spiral Tech" — and the "Falkland War /
British Armed Forces" label is seller lore, NOT a military
variant (RESOLVED 2026-10-24, backlog residual lead (c) from the
non-US Gillette dating run).**
- SPIRAL = the handle. "Spiral Tech" is the collector name for
the English-made Tech with the lightweight, spiral-knurled
aluminum handle that succeeded the knurled ball-end handle:
B&B 660039 ("the knurled ball-end handle that Gillette used
prior to the lightweight spiral handle"; eBay grooming sets
"usually contain an aluminium spiral tech or a ball end
tech"); B&B Twist-To-Open Exploration p.8 calls one "the
1964 Made in England Spiral Tech". The ball-end → spiral
transition sits late 1950s–early 1960s (B&B 660039 #2: a
~1958–1961 English Tech with the newly introduced cylinder-end
handle in regular knurling "made in small quantities before
they changed the knurling"). Dataset strings confirm posters
use "Spiral" as a handle descriptor: "1967 Gilette British
Spiral Tech (M3)", "1972 Spiral Tech", "Gillette 1960s
British Ball-End Tech Spiral Handle" (handle swaps and
grooming-set pairings are common in this family — see B&B
660039 — so a spiral handle on a ball-end-era head is a
pairing, not a model).
- FALKLAND half — dataset profile (sotd_2020–2021_full.json):
12 hits, TWO authors only — Sleezey-Sleeze 11×
("1980's Gillette British Armed Forces Falkland War Spiral
Tech", Apr 2020–Jun 2021) and nonsenseofsight 1× ("1985
Gillette Falkland Spiral Tech", May 2021, a birthday gift
from his dad). Neither note gives provenance; Sleezey's
first note says only "Got my Tech in yesterday" (Apr 24,
2020) — consistent with a fresh online purchase carrying a
seller's description. (The backlog's "6 hits" counted the
base string only; the full variant count is 12.)
- VERDICT: no evidence anywhere of a Falklands War / British
Armed Forces issue of the Spiral Tech. Forum + web search
finds British military Techs ONLY in the WWII era (a
1943-dated, broad-arrow-marked British military issue razor,
B&B 74422 #29; WWII contract Techs; US GI Techs), and by the
1980s troops were getting disposables (B&B boot-camp 1989
account: a ten-pack of cheap disposables). Genuine MOD-issue
kit carries the broad arrow + year (B&B 74422 #23); no
broad-arrow Spiral Tech has been documented anywhere. The
"1980's"/"1985" dating is the posters' own dating of a
late-production civilian English Tech — the spiral-handle
family certainly ran into the 1970s (B&B: "1970s English
Tech... very light"), and nonsenseofsight's "Super light"
matches the aluminum spiral handle exactly.
- CATALOG GUIDANCE [catalog]: fold "Falkland Spiral Tech" /
"British Armed Forces Falkland War Spiral Tech" strings into
the English Spiral Tech (late aluminum Tech) family — the
Falkland/Armed-Forces words are provenance lore, not
identity. Do NOT create a military-issue entry, and do NOT
cite "1980's"/"1985" in these strings as established
production dates (poster dating only). Contrast with the
genuine WWII British military Tech (1943, broad arrow) —
different era, different razor. Pattern note: seller/poster
provenance stories attach to English Tech-family razors
(compare the Personna BBS-0 airline-amenity lead, also
unproven) — provenance words in a dataset string are never
identity without a mark (broad arrow, packaging) to back
them.
- Confidence: established for Spiral = spiral-knurled aluminum
handle on English Techs; established-negative for the
Falkland military claim (no source found across forum + web
search; the claim rests on two posters, one razor each).
Sources: badgerandblade.com threads 660039, 655499 p.8,
