*Confidence: established · Source: [[hardware|Hardware lore]]*

**[catalog] Non-US Gillette dating — British and Canadian systems
(RESOLVED 2026-10-24, backlog follow-up to the US date-code
run).** The US letter+digit table above does NOT transfer
wholesale outside the US. The two non-US systems differ from
each other, and from the US system, in opposite directions:
- ENGLAND — mostly NO date codes at all. The community
consensus (B&B's AsylumGuido: "Date codes didn't start
appearing on British Gillettes until the 80's"; vintageshaving.au
on Rockets: "Gillette England did not date code their razors
like Gillette in America") is that classic-era British razors
are dated by MODEL, FEATURES, PATENT numbers, and SET numbers
— never by a code, because usually there is none. Absence of
a code on a British razor is NORMAL, not missing data, and
must never be treated as a dating failure. Dating proxies,
all established by collector consensus: Rocket family =
post-war, as early as 1948, into the 1960s, dated by comparison
to the lookalike US Super Speed (the Rocket gap — the TTO knob
shortens and recedes into the handle on opening — plus solid
post and pointed side plates identify an English TTO vs a US
one); British Aristocrats = 4 generations over ~30 years,
dated by generation features (Gen 1 open comb, mid-late 1930s,
PAT 400.621 then 430.030; Gen 2 late 1940s solid bar; Gen 3
late 1940s/early 1950s, heaviest at 82 g; Gen 4 1953–58ish,
diamond-pressed baseplate = same head as the era's Rockets);
British PAT numbers on the baseplate (e.g. 694093 on Rockets,
403030 on Aristocrat Jr./#48) are patent markers, not dates.
SET-NUMBER CAUTION [catalog]: the often-cited Aristocrat
numbers (#15, #16, #19, #20, #21, #22, #48, #49, #58) and
British NEW numbers (#77) refer to full SETS (razor + case),
not to razors — a bare razor has no #, only a generation.
Never create a "#16" or "#21" razor model entry; the dataset
strings "British Aristocrat #16" (147 hits) and "UK #21"
are set citations.
- ENGLAND, CODED EXCEPTIONS (the "no codes" rule is a strong
tendency, not an absolute): some British razors DO carry
letter+digit codes, and when they do, posters read them on
the US cycle — "Gillette Super Slim Twist, made in England
U2 (=1974)" (dataset, Teufelskraft 2022, poster-decoded);
"British Aluminum Handle Gillette tech A1" (dataset,
MajorMinceMeat 2020); B&B firsthand: a British Tech with P3
read as 1970 Q3, a "Y date code ('53)" Tech, an L2 ('66)
travel Tech. So a code on a British razor is usable evidence
(read on the US cycle), but a British razor WITHOUT a code
proves nothing about its date. The blanket "no British codes
until the 80s" claim is therefore recorded as SUPERSEDED in
its absolute form — the 80s claim survives only as "systematic
British coding is a late phenomenon; coded pre-80s British
razors are exceptions."
- CANADA — its own early serial system, then the US code
system. Early (Montreal plant, from ~1905–06): serials with a
C prefix for Single Rings and a PC prefix for Pocket Editions
(PC 5646, PC 06227, PC 07509 in the B&B Canada-serials
thread; C 10286, C 72999), later dropping the prefix; the
PC 5646 ≈ June 1909 mapping is a single-thread estimate
(Peterson, Gillette Blade Canadian Edition, July 1918, cited
there) — lead, not established. Post-1950 Canadian razors
carry US-system letter+digit codes, dataset-verified:
"Gillette Canadian Tech X4" (6 hits), "Tech (Canada) W2
(1951)" (poster-decoded), "1953 Y4 Canadian Fat Handle
Tech" — all consistent with the US cycle, so Canadian
post-1950 codes MAY be read on the US table. PATENT CAUTION
[catalog]: "Canada Pat 1932" / "PAT Canada 1932" / "Pat'd
1932 - Canada" on Canadian Techs is a PATENT year, not a
manufacture date — it marks the famous pre-war Tech
(triangle/oval slots, "Canada 1932" in dataset strings), which
is a prized variant in its own right, not a 1932-dated razor.
The same caution applies to "Made in Canada" Techs generally:
origin tag first, code second.
- PLANT-LETTER SYSTEM — RESOLVED 2026-11-05 (backlog residual
lead (a) from the non-US Gillette dating run; CORROBORATED,
scope corrected). **CORRECTION to the earlier guess:** this is
NOT a later/cartridge-era mark family — it is the
**1921–1930 New Improved serial-number era**, number-then-
letter format (e.g. 123456-Y). The trailing letter is the
COUNTRY OF MANUFACTURE: **V = Spain, W = France, Y =
England, Z = Canada**; the US run used the same format with
series letters **A (1921–22), B (1922–27), C (1928–29)**
(year-banded; serial gap Oct 18, 1927–Aug 27, 1928). Two
published sources now agree verbatim: Shave Dad's guide
(read live 2026-11-05): "A trailing letter marks the plant
instead of the year. Y is England, Z is Canada, V is Spain
and W is France, and none of the US tables on this page
apply to those"; and shaveopedia's Gillette serial table
(read live 2026-11-05): "SPAIN - from #1-V to #999.999-V |
FRANCE - from #1-W to #999.999-W | ENGLAND - from #1-Y to
#999.999-Y | CANADA - from #1-Z to #999.999-Z", inside the
New Improved section, with the US -A/-B/-C series in the same
table. LINEAGE CAVEAT: both descend from the same collector
literature (Robert K. Waits' "Safety Razor Reference Guide";
the B&B US wiki's serial ranges match shaveopedia's exactly,
though the B&B wiki itself carries NO foreign rows) — two
publications, one lineage; no Gillette-internal document has
surfaced, so this is established at collector-consensus level.
**SCOPE RULES [catalog]:** (1) the plant letter gives COUNTRY
only, never a year — the foreign series run 1–999,999 with NO
year bands in any source (contrast the US A/B/C series, which
ARE year-banded, so a US New Improved serial dates the razor
and a foreign one does not, without a per-country table that
does not exist); (2) it applies ONLY to the 1921–1930
number-letter serials — never to the 1950–1988 letter+digit
date codes, where the letter is the YEAR; (3) SHARED-LETTER
TRAP: V, W, Y, Z mean years in the date-code system (V =
1950/1975, W = 1951/1976, Y = 1953/1978, Z = 1954/1979) AND
countries in the serial system (V = Spain, W = France, Y =
England, Z = Canada) — the mark's FORMAT (letter+digit vs
long-number+letter) decides which system applies, never the
letter alone; (4) no trailing-letter Germany row exists in
either source — the Leicester-era leading-letter G = Germany
hypothesis (see the early-British-serial lead below) is a
DIFFERENT, earlier system (letter-FIRST, 1909–1915), never
to be merged with this one; (5) the 1930 De Luxe serials
(1-D…) are a separate US-only family. **DATASET FOOTPRINT:
ZERO** — a tight full-corpus check 2026-11-05 found no SOTD
poster citing any New Improved serial number at all (posters
date these razors by year/model, e.g. "NEW Improved 1929",
"Big Fellow (1926)"); this is validator dating lore, not a
matcher rule — no entry or alias action owed. Confidence:
established (two published sources in verbatim agreement,
internally coherent table), with the single-lineage caveat
above. Sources: shavedad.com/gillette-date-codes-serial-
numbers/; shaveopedia.com/shaveopedia/Gillette/Gillette%20USA
%20serial%20numbers.htm. NEW NARROW LEADS: (a1) a direct
read of Waits' "Safety Razor Reference Guide" serial table
(or any Gillette-internal document) to firm the lineage
caveat; (a2) per-country year bands for foreign New Improved
serials — does any serial census exist (Canadian/English
collector threads)?; (a3) Germany's absence from the
trailing-letter table — was there a German New Improved
serial run, or did Germany get no trailing-letter series?
- EARLY BRITISH SERIALS (lead, explicitly hedged by its own
authors): the B&B British-single-ring thread reconstructs
Leicester-plant (operational late 1908/early 1909, closed
~1915–16) serials as E/F/G/H prefixes ("made in" Britain)
vs A/B prefixes ("made for" the British market, i.e. Boston
or Montreal production shipped to London), with Montreal
supplying Britain after Leicester closed. The thread's own
authors call this speculation ("semi-educated guess, at least
as likely to be wrong as right") — record as a lead only.
- DATASET USAGE (verified 2026-10-24 across sotd_2016–2026):
British strings 209 distinct / 572 hits cite generations,
set numbers, "flat-bottom made in England", and PAT numbers
— essentially never codes (the only code-like tokens in
British strings are the two genuine exceptions above plus
multi-razor list artifacts like "S1 Atlas" / "V3 Head"
belonging to other razors in the same field). Canadian
strings 73 distinct / 147 hits cite codes (X4, W2, Y4),
"Canada 1932" patent tags, and "Made in Canada" origin tags.
Rocket strings 96 distinct / 358 hits date by year or range
("c.1949", "1948–1954", "Rocket MD 1953") — model-comparison
dating, exactly as the no-code account predicts.
MATCHER RULES [catalog]: (1) never apply the US table to a
British razor merely because it is a Gillette — require a
visible code; (2) a British "no code" is not a variant;
(3) "Canada 1932" is a patent tag, never a manufacture year;
(4) Aristocrat/NEW set numbers (#16, #21, #77…) are set
citations, never razor models; (5) "Rocket" without a
qualifier is the English TTO family, dated 1948–1960s by
comparison, not by code.
Confidence: established for the England-no-code tendency, the
Canadian post-1950 US-cycle codes, the set-number rule, the
dataset patterns, and now the 1921–1930 trailing-letter
plant system (V/W/Y/Z = Spain/France/England/Canada; see the
resolved entry above, single-lineage caveat noted); lead for
the early British serial reconstruction. Sources: badgerandblade.com
forum threads 71718 (AsylumGuido), 24758, 396922 (British
single rings), 355936 + posts/4368683 (Canada serials),
599541 (British Aristocrat sets); vintageshaving.au/razors/
gillette_rocket_part_one/; shavedad.com/
gillette-date-codes-serial-numbers/ (plant-letter claim);
