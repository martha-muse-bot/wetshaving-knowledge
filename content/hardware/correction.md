**CORRECTION — Knockout is NOT Souplex, and the
National/Nelson/Knockout/Fuluse family is a shared DESIGN,
not a shared maker [catalog].** This corrects the shorthand
in the 2026-10-25 Marspen addendum ("Souplex: Big Ben, Double
Six, National/Knockout, Nymph"), which wrongly grouped
Knockout under Souplex.
- National IS Souplex: Owners Club #144 (thread-starter
opinion, corroborated by handling): "Souplex went on to
produce the identical National." National↔Nelson handle
interchangeability is firsthand (#107: "the Souplex
National handle IS interchangeable with the Nelson —
which is the same razor again but in brown").
- Nelson = the same razor in brown; maker "Harrison Fisher"
per #144 — single-source attribution, LEAD, not established.
- Knockout is a Cartledge commission made by Cooper & Sons:
B&B "Got a Knockout" thread 157951 #34 (firsthand): the
Knockout is "IDENTICAL to the Fuluse" razor "apart from
the colour," "even the shape of the box and the picture of
the razor on it are exactly the same"; Fuluse was made by
Cooper & Sons of the Lockfast Works, Sheffield, and F. C.
Cartledge "was an entrepreneur (and who primarily sold
postcards and trading cards with a boxing theme) rather
than a cutler, so it is a good guess that he commissioned
Cooper & Sons to make his razors." Catalog: Knockout gets
a Cartledge/Cooper entry, NEVER a Souplex fold.
- The moulding theory (#144, thread-starter opinion only):
Cooper, a metalworker/blade maker, likely lacked plastics
expertise; "the only firms with the expertise in plastics
at that time are probably United Plastics ... and
Souplex," and Souplex's later identical National "points
firmly in their direction." So Souplex is the PLAUSIBLE
Bakelite moulder behind the family design — but that is a
theory about who moulded Fuluse/Knockout shells, NOT
evidence that Knockout is a Souplex product. Keep the
distinction: shared design family, separate makers.
- Handling corroboration that the runs differ (#109,
firsthand): the Knockout handle does NOT fit the National
or Nelson — "the Knockout cap thread is slightly different
from the other two. Although all three are ostensibly the
same razor." Different threads = different production,
consistent with separate makers.
