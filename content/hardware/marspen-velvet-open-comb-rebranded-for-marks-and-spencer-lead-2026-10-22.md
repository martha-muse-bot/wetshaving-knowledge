*Confidence: established · Source: [[Hardware lore]]*

**Marspen = Velvet open comb rebranded for Marks & Spencer
(lead, 2026-10-22).** Semaj3000 2022: "my Marspen razor, which is a
Velvet open comb rebranded for the British department store Marks
and Spencer." Single source. Lead.

**Addendum 2026-10-25 — M&S half ESTABLISHED, "Velvet" half
UNCORROBORATED, Sheffield-clone family as the better-supported
framing [catalog].** Dataset scope pinned first: exactly TWO
Marspen posts in all years, both Semaj3000, 2022 — razor field
"Marspen open comb" (2022-02-08, no note) and the same string
2022-04-14 with the full note verbatim: "Used my Marspen razor,
which is a Velvet open comb rebranded for the British department
store Marks and Spencer. It's the usual early 20th century open
comb design and it's a vicious little razor for sure!" So the
firsthand facts are: open comb, early-20th-century pattern,
aggressive shaver.
- Marspen = M&S own brand is ESTABLISHED on a primary source:
the M&S Archive's advertising/branding timeline lists Marspen
among the early own-brand names (with Monster, Welbeck, Daymar)
used on goods from c.1900–1920s, and its homeware timeline
repeats Marspen as an own brand; the name was still an M&S
own-brand in the 1940s (the Marspen series of shaped toy books).
The store-brand half of Semaj3000's claim is therefore confirmed
independently of him.
- The Sheffield-clone family framing (single hedged B&B source,
Souplex Owners Club thread 636564 p.6 #104): "These Gillette old
type clones were produced in Sheffield for many companies and
stores over a very long period of time. That 'Best British' looks
like a dept store version, maybe a later Marspen (Marks and
Spencer) when they dropped the cap branding? (30s/40s?)
Apparently Wilkinson Sword produced that one but they all look
of Laurel origin to me." Both halves hedged ("maybe", "apparently",
"look ... to me") — LEAD, not established — but it places Marspen
razors inside the Sheffield Old-Type-clone family (Wilkinson
Sword as claimed producer of that example; George H. Lawrence's
Laurel as the look-alike origin), which fits Semaj3000's
"usual early 20th century open comb design" better than a
distinct "Velvet" maker does.
- "Velvet" as the underlying brand: NEGATIVE CHECK 2026-10-25 —
no "Velvet" DE safety-razor brand surfaces in any web search
(Sheffield lists, Waits-compendium-adjacent sources, vintage
listings); the only "Velvet razor" hits are a barber's hone
("The Velvet Razor Hone") and velvet-lined cases. Semaj3000's
"Velvet" is therefore a single-source claim with no second
source: it may be a real obscure Sheffield trade name, a
misreading of a cap stamp, or his own shorthand — UNPINNED.
Do NOT record Velvet as a manufacture attribute and do NOT
fold Marspen into any Velvet entry. [[Catalog guidance [catalog]]]:
Marspen stands as its own razor entry (M&S store brand, open
comb); the catalog currently holds NO Marspen entry, so
validators create one rather than aliasing these strings
elsewhere. Re-open the Velvet question only on a cap-stamp
photo or a second owner naming Velvet.

**Addendum 2026-10-25 — Sheffield Old-Type-clone family, PARTIALLY
RESOLVED: the family is real as a pattern, not as a proven maker
chain; Laurel firms up, Wilkinson weakens, Souplex leaves the
family [catalog].**
- Dataset scope pinned first: "Best British" has ZERO razor-field
strings in sotd_2016–2026 (the two text hits are false
positives: "best British accent" and "best British soap") — Best
British is currently a forum-identified example only, not a
dataset product. "Laurel open comb" = 23 razor-field hits, ALL
one author (Enndeegee, 2020–2022) — a real maker, but a
single-author dataset string, below the 3-author attestation
bar. Souplex = 3 razor-field strings / 3 authors (Semaj3000
"Souplex Open Comb"; Scorpio93x frankenrazor; ischiapp
"Souplex Nymph Lady").
- Best British as a BRAND (B&B Souplex thread 636564 p.6 #103,
same thread as the #104 family claim, second voice): the
under-guard branding "Best British" is the brand name, in two
tiers — a cheap litho-tin version ("poxy handle, cheaper
guard, cap ok just") and "fancy pants heavy brass silver
plate editions in round bakelite cases," with only two handle
types seen. So a future Best British string is a brand, not an
adjective — but the two-tier split means tier must be recorded
from the example, never assumed.
- Laurel as a maker is now ESTABLISHED well beyond the Waits
citation: George Herbert Lawrence Ltd, Nursery Street /
"Laurel Works," Sheffield. Picture Sheffield (Sheffield
Libraries archive): Lawrence was a razor manufacturer with a
Nursery Street factory; in 1940 he gave one million Laurel
razors to French troops; he was killed when his factory was
destroyed in the Sheffield Blitz (Dec 1940). A local-history
page (chrishobbs.com, descendant-letter sourced) adds: born
1888, first razors gold-plated, Laurel brand launched with a
Laurel & Hardy Sheffield visit; the works were rebuilt in
1948, still "Laurel Works," and the business continued until
the early 1970s. Waits (via Wegian Wetshaving) records the
Laurel "Dumb Bell" Razor (1934) and the scaled-down Ladies
Boudoir (1935–1940 per a seller listing). Laurel was thus a
large-scale Sheffield DE maker across the 1930s–1970s — the
"Laurel origin" of the family look is PLAUSIBLE as a dominant
local pattern, but this still does NOT prove Laurel made Best
British or Marspen razors. Look-alike ≠ manufacture.
- The Wilkinson-produced half is now the WEAKEST leg, not
strengthened: Wilkinson Sword was a LONDON maker (founded
1772 Shotley Bridge/London; UK plants London, Cramlington,
Bridgend per Wikipedia — Sheffield is not among them), and its
early safety razors were SINGLE-EDGE (Pall Mall 1896, Empire;
encyclopedia.com timeline + Throughout History restoration
write-up), not DE open-comb Old Type clones. A Sheffield DE
open comb "produced by Wilkinson Sword" would need Wilkinson
to have made a DE open comb in the 1930s/40s — no such product
surfaced in this run. Keep the #104 "Apparently Wilkinson
Sword produced that one" as a hedged single-source claim;
do NOT upgrade it, and do NOT attribute any Best British /
Marspen example to Wilkinson on it.
- The Marspen→Best British link stays pure speculation: #104's
"maybe a later Marspen when they dropped the cap branding?
(30s/40s?)" has no supporting evidence — nothing shows Marspen
razors continuing into the 1930s/40s or dropping cap branding.
Keep Marspen and Best British as SEPARATE entries; re-open a
link only on a cap-stamp photo or a dated Marspen example
post-1930.
- CORRECTION to the family boundary: Souplex is NOT a Sheffield
maker and does NOT belong in the Sheffield family. V&A museum
record (accession M.59-1984): Big Ben is "a brand name used
for razors by Souplex, a manufacturer based in Morecambe,
Lancashire," c.1935. English Old-Type-lookalike razors
therefore come from at least TWO centres — Sheffield (Laurel
and the store-brand clones) and Morecambe (Souplex: Big Ben,
Double Six, National/Knockout, Nymph) — and similarity across
centres is pattern convergence, not a shared maker. Do NOT
fold Souplex strings into the Sheffield family, and do NOT
use a Souplex example to date or attribute a Sheffield one.
- [[Catalog guidance]] [catalog]: (1) Marspen, Best British, and
Laurel each stand as their OWN razor entries; no merges among
them on current evidence. (2) "Sheffield Old-Type clone" is a
useful FAMILY DESCRIPTOR for notes, never a manufacture
attribute — validators must not cite it as provenance.
(3) A future Best British string needs its tier (litho-tin
cheap vs heavy brass silver-plate) recorded from a photo;
a future Laurel string is single-author until a second owner
surfaces.

**Addendum 2026-10-26 — Enndeegee's "Laurel open comb": model
narrowed to the Dumb Bell by elimination (LEAD, not established);
second owner still none [catalog].**
- Dataset scope re-verified this run: "Laurel open comb" = 23
razor-field hits, ALL one author (Enndeegee), 2020-09-01 to
2022-12-14, strings `laurel open comb` (18) / `Laurel open
comb` (5). Still single-author — below the 3-author
attestation bar, so the string stays a single-owner
observation, not an attested product.
- Blade evidence from the same posts is decisive on one axis:
Enndeegee shaved it with standard modern DE blades only —
7 O'Clock Yellow (8), Feather (5), Gillette Silver Blue
(6 incl. spellings), Rapira Swedish Steel (2), Voskhod (1).
It takes standard DE blades. That RULES OUT the Ladies
Boudoir (miniature, proprietary tiny blades, slotted guard
with explicitly NO comb teeth — Saffron Walden Museum
object record + Wegian) as the identity of his razor.
- The only documented full-size Laurel DE is the Dumb Bell
(1934 per Waits via Wegian). New primary-ad evidence this
run: Geo. H. Lawrence Ltd advertised the "LAUREL DUMB BELL
RAZOR" (nickel-plated, 6d) in The Chemist and Druggist on
Sept 3, 1938 and again June 17, 1939 (Wellcome Collection
scans of the same ad run; Reg. No. 794-132), alongside the
gold-plated Ladies Boudoir — so the Dumb Bell was in current
retail at least 1938–39, not a one-year curiosity.
- Why this is still a LEAD, not established: no source found
describes the Dumb Bell's head as open-comb, and the B&B
Sheffield thread (505224 #16) complicates the blade story —
its author was "jumping through hoops getting some Laurel
Dumb-Bell blades" and states "The Wardonia, Knockout, Laurel
and Ocean blades are all interchangeable." If Dumb-Bell
blades were a proprietary Sheffield family, Enndeegee's
standard-blade razor is either a Dumb Bell that also accepts
standard blades, or a different (undocumented) full-size
Laurel open comb. The interchangeability claim may itself
just describe shared standard 3-hole blades — unpinned.
Do NOT record "Laurel open comb = Dumb Bell" as established
until a Dumb Bell head photo or Waits entry confirms an open
comb and standard-blade fit.
- Firsthand shave character (single owner, Enndeegee's notes):
"riding the cap is working really well"; "ideal angle is
smaller on the Laurel" than on his Gillette Old Type
(2021-05-05); "the laurel feels more like the OLD type than
the NEW" (2022-12-03); compared directly against his BBS-0.
Useful color for a future entry; single-source, never
quoted as consensus.
- Catalog guidance [catalog]: keep "Laurel open comb" as its
own Laurel razor entry (NOT the Boudoir, NOT folded into
Marspen/Best British); record the Dumb Bell as the
LEAD model identification with this addendum as the
citation; validators: a second owner's Laurel open comb
would lift the string past single-author, and a Dumb Bell
head photo would settle the model question outright.

**Addendum 2026-10-26 — Wilkinson Sword DE open-comb hunt:
RESOLVED as far as text research can go — the #104 claim is
UNCORROBORATED and now weighted UNLIKELY; do NOT attribute
any Best British / Marspen example to Wilkinson [catalog].**
- The claim, verbatim (B&B Souplex Owners Club 636564 p.6
#104, same post as the family framing): "Apparently
Wilkinson Sword produced that one but they all look of
Laurel origin to me." Two hedges in one sentence, no
marking, catalog, or example cited — and the claimant's
own lean is Laurel, not Wilkinson. It was never more
than a single hedged source; this run finds nothing to
lift it.
- Wilkinson's own-brand razor history, as documented, has
NO DE open comb in it at any era: Pall Mall (1898, hollow
single-edge wedge blade; encyclopedia.com timeline +
Charleston Museum kit note) and Empire (c.1929–1952,
adjustable SE wedge, B&B 260051) are both single-edge;
the 1970 Bonded system is a cartridge (B&B 610295);
the modern Classic is a closed-comb TTO/3-piece DE
(German-made, Edgewell). Targeted searches ("Wilkinson
Sword open comb razor", "Wilkinson Sword double edge
razor vintage made in England", "Best British razor
Wilkinson produced") return no Wilkinson DE open comb —
no product, no listing, no second collector naming one.
- CORRECTION to the 2026-10-25 addendum above: its "London
maker, Sheffield is not among its plants" line is too
flat. The Springate/Goins-derived straight-razor
manufacturer lists (razorarchive.com, iknifecollector.com)
DO place "WILKINSON SWORD CO LTD" at Arundel Lane,
Sheffield — so Wilkinson had a Sheffield cutlery
presence in the straight-razor era. That does NOT
rehabilitate the #104 claim: the listing is a
straight-razor-era directory entry, not evidence of a
Sheffield DE-razor factory, and Wikipedia's plant list
(London Chelsea/Acton, Cramlington, Bridgend) covers
the company's manufacturing sites. A Sheffield address
for a cutler ≠ a Sheffield DE open-comb production line.
- What Wilkinson DID make in DE, later: the 1960s "Sticky"
(dataset: "Wilkinson Sticky" / "Wilkinson Sword Razor
(aka Sticky)", 2019–2025 strings; "1960s Wilkinson Sticky"
dating from posters; a 2020 note credits the design to
Sir Kenneth Grange — the Design Museum's Grange
retrospective confirms Grange designed Wilkinson Sword
razors over decades, though it does not name the Sticky
specifically). The Sticky proves Wilkinson made DE
razors — in the 1960s, under its own name — not that it
jobbed 1930s/40s open combs for dept stores. Its comb
type is unpinned from this run;
do NOT cite it as open-comb.
- Catalog guidance [catalog]: keep the #104 Wilkinson
sentence as a single hedged community claim, never as a
manufacture attribute; Best British and Marspen stay
unattributed on maker (Laurel-look pattern only).
RE-OPEN ONLY on a cap-stamp photo naming Wilkinson, or
a Wilkinson catalog/ad listing a DE open comb. The
Sticky gets its own Wilkinson razor entry (nickname
"Sticky", 1960s, Grange-design lead) — it is NOT the
#104 razor and must not be cited in that thread.
