**Addendum 2026-11-06 — Double Six launch PINNED, Nelson maker
UPGRADED, German-origin lead, moulder theory strengthened
[catalog]:**
- **Double Six launch — RESOLVED, split: BLADE 1929, RAZOR
1934.** (a) The Double Six BLADE was "NEW" in the C&D Nov 30,
1929 Christmas ad (read verbatim: "THE NEW, DOUBLE SIX
BLADE. 8/3 per box of 1 gross" alongside the Souplex blade
Christmas copy; wholesale 20/- per box of 120). (b) The
Double Six Combless RAZOR was advertised Aug 4, 1934 (C&D
p.10, read verbatim): "DOUBLE-SIX Combless — The
revolutionary design of this safety razor alone is sufficient
to ensure easy sales... replaces the old-fashioned comb.
Cones hold the blade with rigidity yet prevent hair and soap
clogging the edge. All metal parts are heavily plated,
bakelite. 48/- per gross nett. Millions already sold."
(c) The razor patent was filed ~April 1934: B&B Owners Club
p.8 (thread-starter, 2023) — Dominion Steel Corp's blade
patent was "filed in November 1934, seven months after
Souplex filed the patent for a razor with... 4 corner
projections" — the 4-corner-projection razor patent is the
Double Six's (Pat. No. 433166, carried on 2nd-gen razors).
The pre-1944 bound in this entry is superseded: Double Six
blade = 1929+, Double Six razor = 1934+.
- **Generation rundown (B&B Owners Club p.7, 2023-02-19,
collector's own-markings survey — single-author, treat dates
as collector-grade):** 1st gen Double Six early–mid 1930s
(Regd. No. 791669; UF ivory, PF walnut); 2nd gen ~1936–late
30s/early 40s (Pat. No. 433166; new mould, shorter/wider
pegs); 3rd gen early–mid 40s (Gillette Tech-style guard;
boxes marked 1944; metal-cap version with log/thin pins; UF
disappears from consumer plastics). Double Six Minor 1st gen
mid-40s through 50s (metal cap, same baseplate, drilled
cylindrical holes); 2nd gen late 50s/60s; 3rd gen 60s? —
"Maybe the last Souplex razor?" Handle styles: 1930s–40s
sharp ridge; 1950s–60s round ridge. Post-WW2 refresh
speculation (same author): "The National replaces Double Six
Combless, Double Six Minor replaces Double Six and maybe the
Minor replaces the open comb, matching the three Souplex
blade brands?" — the three blade brands = Souplex / Double
Six / Big Ben (Punch Sept 16, 1942 ad: "thousands more men
will want Souplex, Double Six and Big Ben razor blades —
after the war").
- **Firm end date — NARROWED to the 1960s, still unpinned:**
the Minor 3rd gen ("60s?") is the latest production signal;
the pepper-pot "late 50s(ish)" tail stands; no closure or
dissolution record found (Companies House free service holds
dissolved records only since Jan 2010 — a pre-2010
dissolution is not searchable there; no "SOUPLEX LIMITED"
hit). Do NOT cite a closure year.
- **Nelson maker — UPGRADED to ESTABLISHED: Harrison Fisher &
Co Ltd, Sheffield.** Hawley Sheffield Knives firm history
(hawleysheffieldknives.com): firm began 1897 at Trafalgar
Works; founder Harrison Fisher (1871–1907); interwar
trademark = Lord Nelson picture + 'TRAFALGAR'; in 1930 the
firm marketed penny safety razor blades — 'Trafalgar Razor
Blade', slogan 'Clear the Face as Nelson Cleared the Seas'.
Renamed Taylor's Eye Witness Ltd in 2007. The Nelson razor —
the same-design family in brown (B&B firsthand handle
interchangeability with the National) — now has a named maker
with matching Nelson branding and a 1930 blade-market entry.
Catalog: Nelson → Harrison Fisher & Co Ltd entry
(same-design family); the razor itself is still
design-family-linked via handling evidence, not a maker
catalogue.
- **German-origin story — NEW LEAD (single collector source,
wrapper study):** maborek (Polish blade-wrapper collector,
"GB SOUPLEX" sheet, 2023-11-14): "SOUPLEX is old mark of
razor blades. First blades was produced in Germany, but early
from Germany was exported only steel tape. First blades
imported to UK was made (cut and sharpened) in Sweden; first
wrappers was printed in Germany." Wrapper stamp texts:
"GERMANY make — for SOUPLEX in MORECAMBE, ENGLAND";
"SOUPLEX for UK made in SWEDEN (printed in Germany)";
"blade made in UK (printed in Germany — the same wrapper as
Swedish, but with sticker)". "Due to 'war of quality
domination' between England and Germany — English trade mark
was changed." Variants S113–S122 catalogued (green/blue
frames). And: "SOUPLEX produced razor blades in Germany,
Great Britain, Belgium, Austria, Sweden and France for many
years; in begin of 2000'th in Germany was produced Souplex
blades (in plastic dispensers)" + "SOUPLEX produced NYMPH —
blades for depilation." Reading: the Morecambe firm likely
began as the English distributor of a German blade mark
(post-WWI), then became a UK maker; the SOUPLEX blade brand
itself outlived the Morecambe firm by decades in Germany. All
lead-grade until a second source (German trademark record,
retailer listing, or a second wrapper study) corroborates —
but the wrapper stamp texts are primary-adjacent evidence, so
keep the lead warm, not cold.
- **Moulder theory — STRENGTHENED, and a Knockout dissent to
record:** B&B Owners Club p.8 (same thread-starter, later
post): "I don't buy the idea that Cooper's are making the
razors (for themselves or Harrison Fisher (Nelson) or
Cartledge). They're a metalworkers and blade manufacturer.
The only firms with the expertise in plastics at that time
are probably United Plastics (who did do some work for
Sheffield razor firms at the time) and Souplex." — the United
Plastics leg gains its first specific claim (did work for
Sheffield razor firms), still single-voice. The same poster
later asserts "Yep, Souplex definitely manufacturered the
knockout. There's also seems to be a big connection between
Souplex and those using Dominion Steel blanks." DISSENT NOTE:
this conflicts with the 2026-10-26 resolution (Knockout =
Cartledge commission, Cooper & Sons maker, on the
Fuluse-identity firsthand evidence). Reconciliation, not
overturn: if Souplex MOULDED the Knockout shells for
Cooper/Cartledge, both claims are true at different levels —
Souplex as moulder/manufacturer, Cartledge/Cooper as
brand-of-record. Brand-of-record stays Cartledge/Cooper;
"Souplex-manufactured" is now a strengthened single-voice
lead, never an established manufacture attribute. Also new
from p.8: "I bet Cooper & Sons sold Fuluse brand double six
razors before the war like Pyrota (wonder if Pyrota DE blades
have the same design?)" — Pyrota = another pre-war brand on
the Double Six design (MoDiP holds a Pyrota-branded two-tone
travel set, per p.7); dataset has ZERO Pyrota strings; new
cross-reference lead.
- **Differential-aggression mechanism refined (B&B 383550 p.2,
firsthand measurement):** on a 1944-shipper Double Six, blade
gap is IDENTICAL both sides, as is blade protrusion from the
top cap — the difference is the GUARD: the milder side has
~50% more space between table and blade edge; the cap is
symmetric, the baseplate asymmetric (one side wider). Record
the mechanism as guard-protrusion, not gap/exposure.
- **Souplex as supplier (private-label) — firmed:** p.7
thread-starter: "They're more a supplier than consumer
facing brand like Wardonia" + p.8: "I had always assumed it
was Souplex filling a void left by Laurel after it was bombed
in 1940 (they did so many brand razors)". The
Laurel-bombed-1940 claim is single-voice assumption — parked
as a Laurel-entry lead (see the Laurel section), not
established. The supplier framing is corroborated by the 1934
C&D ad's "Millions already sold" blade volume and the
Dominion Steel Corp private-brand blade ad on the same spread
("We are specialists in the production of Safety Razor Blades
to Private Brands — Home or Export", Dominion Steel Corp Ltd,
Sheffield) — Dominion was the blade supplier behind the
4-corner-projection system; Souplex the razor/plastics house.
